FitSphere: Business Requirements 

1. Product Overview and User Roles 

1.1 Product Overview 

FitSphere is a coach-led fitness application where clients log their food intake, body metrics, and workouts against targets set by their coach. 

The application supports personal fitness tracking as well as coach-managed fitness programs. 

The application has a four-tab bottom navigation menu that provides access to Home, Nutrition, Training, and Progress. 

Every main screen displays the selected date, a calendar shortcut, and a profile avatar that opens Account settings. 

A floating “+” button on Home and Nutrition provides quick logging functionality. 

1.2 User Roles 

FitSphere supports three selectable application roles that determine the user's view and available functionality. 

1. Personal Training User 

The Personal Training User uses the application to track their own food intake, body metrics, workouts, and fitness progress. 

The user can access Home, Nutrition, Training, Progress, and Account settings. 

The user tracks their activities against the targets and training plans assigned by their coach, where applicable. 

The user can switch to another available application role through the role selector in their profile. 

2. Head Coach 

A user can activate the Head Coach role through a toggle in their user profile. 

When the Head Coach role is selected, the application switches to the Head Coach view and provides additional coach-management and configuration functionality. 

The Head Coach can add, view, update, and delete Coach records. 

The Head Coach can view the details of coaches under their management. 

The Head Coach can view the details of trainees/users associated with the coaches under their management. 

The Head Coach can manage and oversee the coaches and their associated trainees/users within their hierarchy. 

The Head Coach can switch to another available application role through the role selector in their profile. 

3. Coach 

A user can switch to the Coach view after logging in, provided the Coach role is available to them. 

The Coach view displays the trainees/users directly assigned to that coach. 

The Coach can add, view, update, and delete trainee/user records under their management. 

The Coach can access the details of their assigned trainees/users to manage their fitness programs. 

The Coach can set fitness targets, diet plans, and training plans for their assigned trainees/users. 

The Coach can switch to another available application role through the role selector in their profile. 

1.3 Role-Based Application Views and Management Hierarchy 

The user profile provides a role selector with three roles: Personal Training User, Head Coach, and Coach. 

Users can switch between the roles available to them. The application view and available functionality change according to the currently selected role. 

Selecting the Personal Training User role displays the personal fitness-tracking experience. 

Selecting the Head Coach role displays the Head Coach experience, including coach management and visibility into trainees/users under the managed coaches. 

Selecting the Coach role displays the Coach experience, including management of trainees/users directly assigned to that coach. 

The Head Coach manages Coaches, while each Coach manages their assigned trainees/users. 

Head Coach permissions include CRUD operations on Coach records. 

Coach permissions include CRUD operations on trainee/user records under their management. 

The Head Coach can view the details of both the coaches under their management and the trainees/users associated with those coaches. 

2. Date Handling (Global) 

Users can view any past date using the calendar icon or the week/date strip. All data displayed on the screen follows the selected date. 

When a past date is being viewed, a banner displays that date with a “Back to today” shortcut. 

Today is highlighted in blue on all date strips. 

“Today” and day boundaries follow the user's configured country and time zone. 

3. Home Dashboard 

The header displays the selected date and the user's username. 

A Monday-to-Sunday week strip displays a status box for each day, highlighting both today and the selected day. 

The calories card displays kcal eaten against the target, including a progress bar, percentage achieved, and kcal remaining. 

The protein card displays grams eaten against the target, including a progress bar, percentage achieved, and grams remaining. 

A “By meal” link opens the meal-wise breakdown for the selected day. 

Quick tiles for Weight (kg), Steps, and Sleep display the logged value or “+ Add” when no value has been logged. 

The workout tracker displays workouts completed against the weekly target, for example, “1 of 5 · 4 left,” with Monday-to-Sunday dots marking workout days. 

A Measurements card prompts the user to take weekly waist measurements. 

4. Measurements Logging 

Measurements open as a bottom sheet from the Home card or the Progress screen. 

A date selector defaults to Today and can be changed to log measurements for another date. 

A cm/in toggle allows the user to select the measurement unit. 

Upper waist measurement is mandatory, with the guidance “2 fingers above the navel.” 

Lower waist measurement is mandatory, with the guidance “2 fingers below the navel.” 

An expandable section contains optional fields for chest, hips, arms, thighs, and notes. 

The “Save measurements” button becomes active only after values have been entered. 

A close (X) button exits without saving. 

5. Nutrition: Consumed 

A horizontally scrollable date strip allows users to switch between days. 

Three tabs are available: Consumed, Diet plan, and Saved. 

The Eaten summary displays total kcal and colour-coded Protein, Carbs, Fat, and Fiber values. A dash is displayed when there is no data. 

Meal slots for Breakfast, Lunch, Snack, and Dinner each display “Tap to add what you ate” with a “+” button. 

Each logged meal has a “•••” menu that includes the “Save to my meals” option. 

6. Nutrition: Diet Plan 

A daily target card displays calories and protein, marked “Set by your coach.” These targets are read-only for the client. 

A collapsible “How to use this plan” guide includes a “Got it” dismiss button. 

The guide instructs users to meet their calorie and protein targets, build every meal around a complete protein source, and log every meal. 

Each meal slot has a card displaying its total kcal and protein, for example, Breakfast: 646 kcal, 50 g protein. 

Each meal offers up to three alternatives as tabs: Option 1, Option 2, and Option 3. 

Each option displays the meal name and an ingredient list with quantities in grams, scoops, tablespoons, or pieces. 

The protein source is listed first and highlighted in every option. 

Each option has an action button, partly visible in the screenshot, presumably to log that planned meal. 

7. Nutrition: Saved 

This tab lists the user's saved meals for one-tap re-logging. 

When no meals are saved, an empty state explains how to save a meal from the Consumed tab. 

8. Log Meal 

The Log meal screen opens from any “+” button. It has a back button and a date chip that defaults to Today. 

The date chip opens a “Log this meal to” sheet showing Today, Yesterday, and the previous five days. 

Each date in the sheet displays its logging status, such as “nothing logged,” and an “Another day…” option opens a calendar. 

Meal type options are Breakfast, Lunch, Snack, and Dinner, along with additional tags for Before training and Dessert. 

A free-text box with the placeholder “Speak or type what you ate…” includes a sample entry. 

A “Speak your meal” button allows voice entry. 

A camera button captures a meal photo or nutrition label. 

Alternative logging methods include Search database, Scan barcode, Eaten before (past meals), and Saved (your meals). 

Best-practice tips advise users to state amounts in grams, specify whether food is raw or cooked (for example, rice weighs about three times more cooked), and name each food separately. 

A collapsible Examples section displays Best, Vague, and Say examples, with a Hide option. 

AI converts text, voice, and photo inputs into food items and quantities, subject to the user's AI consent setting. 

9. Training 

The coach-assigned training plan appears day by day, with a how-to guide for every exercise. 

Until a coach sends a plan, an empty state displays “No training plan yet.” 

“Start an empty workout” allows users to log a self-directed session. 

“Workouts you have logged” displays the user's workout history. 

Lifting weights are recorded in the unit selected in Account settings. 

10. Progress 

The “Weight over 4 weeks” card displays weight change in kg, along with the weekly rate. 

The weekly rate requires at least one week of weigh-ins. A message is displayed until sufficient data is available. 

Average calories are displayed against the target. 

Meals logged are displayed as days logged out of the total number of days, for example, “0 of 27 days.” 

The weight trend chart has range filters for 1W, 4W, 12W, 26W, and All. An empty state is displayed until enough weigh-ins exist. 

A week-by-week table displays Body Weight, Calories, Protein, and Steps against a Target row. 

A “Pre” row in the table holds baseline data from before the program. 

Tapping a week opens its details. 

The Measurements section includes an “Add first” button and recommends tracking upper and lower waist measurements weekly. 

11. Account and Settings 

Appearance offers System, Light, and Dark themes, with helper text explaining the selected option. 

Units for lifting weights in workouts and workout history can be set independently to kg or lb. 

Units for body weight in weigh-ins and progress can be set independently to kg or lb. 

An “AI meal reading” toggle controls consent. Users are asked for consent before any meal text, photo, voice note, or nutrition label is sent to AI. 

The food database, barcodes, and saved meals work even when AI is turned off. 

Country and time zone are configurable, for example, India · Kolkata. 

Users can change their password. 

Users can log out. 

“Delete account” opens a separate flow. 

The user profile provides access to the three application roles: Personal Training User, Head Coach, and Coach, subject to the roles available to that user. 

Switching the active role changes the application view and the functionality available to the user.
